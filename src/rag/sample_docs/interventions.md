## chunk_id: intervention_001
source: placeholder — replace with cited source
tags: overlapping-deadlines, prioritisation
When several deadlines overlap, breaking each task into the smallest
sub-steps that can be finished in under an hour, and then sequencing those
sub-steps across the available days rather than trying to complete one
whole assignment before starting the next, tends to reduce the feeling of
being overwhelmed and creates early visible progress on every item.

## chunk_id: intervention_002
source: placeholder — replace with cited source
tags: low-study-time, triage
When available study time is clearly less than the time a set of tasks
requires, an explicit triage step — deciding which task most affects the
final grade or has the least flexible deadline, and consciously
de-prioritising the rest to a "good enough" standard — is more effective
than attempting equal effort on everything and finishing all of it poorly.

## chunk_id: intervention_003
source: placeholder — replace with cited source
tags: sleep, energy, burnout
Consistently reducing sleep below roughly six hours a night to gain extra
study time is associated with reduced next-day concentration and slower
task completion, which can offset or reverse the intended time gain within
two to three days. Protecting a minimum sleep window is often more
time-efficient than it first appears.

## chunk_id: intervention_004
source: placeholder — replace with cited source
tags: part-time-work, fixed-commitments
For students with fixed weekly commitments such as part-time work, planning
study sessions around those fixed blocks (treating them as immovable, like
a lecture) rather than trying to fit work around an idealised free-form
study schedule reduces the number of missed or rescheduled sessions.

## chunk_id: intervention_005
source: placeholder — replace with cited source
tags: untaught-content, self-study
When an assignment requires material not directly covered in lectures,
starting with a short, timeboxed scoping session (e.g. 30 minutes to map out
exactly what needs to be learned and where to find it) before beginning the
task itself prevents the open-ended, anxiety-inducing self-study pattern of
reading broadly without a clear target.

For group assignments layered on top of an already heavy individual
workload, agreeing an internal deadline at least two to three days before
